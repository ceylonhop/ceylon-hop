Thinking about the train?

There’s no station in Sigiriya or Dambulla. The nearest is Habarana, about 20 km away and a few kilometres outside the town itself, with only a couple of useful trains a day — so you’d still need a ride at the end. Almost everyone comes by road. If you’re carrying on to Trincomalee or Batticaloa afterwards, the line east from Habarana is running again; message us and we’ll check the times for your date.

Things to see & do

Seven things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Sigiriya Rock Fortress

King Kashyapa built his palace on top of this rock in the 5th century and a UNESCO World Heritage Site grew around it. The way up passes the water gardens, the painted ladies of the frescoes, the Mirror Wall with 1,000-year-old graffiti, and the two giant Lion’s Paws before the last metal stairs to the summit. The museum by the ticket office is covered by your ticket — do it after, when you’re back in the shade.

2

Pidurangala Rock

The rock next door, and the view of Sigiriya on every postcard. It’s shorter but rougher: a temple at the bottom, a brick reclining Buddha halfway, and a boulder scramble at the top where you’ll use your hands. This is the one for sunrise.

From town3 km · 10 min by tuk-tuk
OpenFrom about 5 AM · temple tickets
Give it1–1.5 hours
EffortModerate–hard · a scramble at the top
TicketRs 1,000 adult · Rs 500 child
Good to knowCover shoulders and knees to pass the temple (they lend a wrap). Wear proper shoes and skip it after heavy rain — the top is bare rock. The last stretch isn’t for anyone who hates heights.
3

Dambulla Cave Temple

Five caves cut into a rock 160 m above the plain, every wall and ceiling painted, and more than 150 Buddha statues inside — including a 14 m reclining Buddha. It’s been a place of worship for over 2,000 years and is still a working temple.

From town20 km · 20–30 min from Sigiriya
Open7 AM–7 PM · tickets until 5 PM
Give it1–1.5 hours
EffortEasy · about 350 steps up
Ticket≈ Rs 3,000 foreign adult · cash
Good to knowBuy your ticket before you climb — the office is by the car park on the main road, not at the Golden Temple and not at the top. Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off at the entrance (bring socks; the rock gets hot). Keep food out of sight of the monkeys.
4

Thalkote lake at sunset

A quieter way to see the rock: a punt or catamaran across Pahala Thalkote Wewa, a village tank a few kilometres out, with Sigiriya reflected in the water and herons on every stump. Some trips add a bullock-cart ride through the paddy fields.

From town6–7 km · 15 min from the rock
OpenLate afternoon is best
Give it1–2 hours
Ticket≈ USD 12–50 per person, by operator
From our driversGo at sunset. Fewer people, the best light on the rock, and it’s the one thing first-timers don’t know to ask for.
5

Minneriya & the elephant gathering

From about July to October, as the reservoirs shrink, hundreds of wild elephants come to graze the grass on the old tank beds — one of the biggest gatherings of Asian elephants anywhere. The herds move between Minneriya, Kaudulla and Hurulu as the water changes, and the jeep drivers know where they are that week.

From townAbout 45 min to the park gates
OpenJeeps go at 6 AM or around 3 PM

From our driversGo in the afternoon — the elephants come out to the grass as it cools. Outside the gathering months you’ll still see elephants most days; ask us which park is best for your dates.
6

A village day

Get off the tourist track for a few hours: a catamaran across a village lake, a walk through the paddy and vegetable plots, and a family lunch cooked over firewood — often a dozen curries in clay pots. The slower, more human side of the area, and good for children.

An Ayurvedic treatment

Sri Lanka’s traditional medicine: warm herbal oils, a proper massage and, if you want it, a consultation with a doctor. The resorts around Sigiriya and Habarana have Ayurveda centres that take outside guests. The best way to recover the day after the climbs.

When to come

The weather, month by month

Sigiriya is in the dry zone: hot all year, with one long dry spell mid-year and its real rain at the end of the year — the opposite of the south coast.

Jan – MarDry & warm

The most comfortable months: clear mornings, 28–32° by day, cooler nights. Peak season — the rock is busiest.

Best for the climb
Apr – MayHottest

April is the hottest month, with afternoon thunderstorms. Climb at opening time and carry more water than you think.

Jun – SepDry & windy

The driest months: hot, clear and breezy. The elephant gathering at Minneriya and Kaudulla peaks in August.

Best for elephants
Oct – DecThe rains

The wettest weeks of the year: heavy afternoon storms, green everything, fewer people. Mornings are usually still fine for the climb.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

How many nights should I stay?

One is enough for Sigiriya and the Dambulla caves. Two lets you add Pidurangala at sunrise, the lake, or an afternoon with the elephants without rushing — and you won’t be doing two climbs in the same heat.

Can I visit as a day trip from Colombo or Kandy?

From Kandy, yes — it’s a long day, 2 h 30 each way. From Colombo it’s over six hours in the car for a few hours at the rock, and you miss the only cool part of the day. Stay a night nearby.

Do I need to book Sigiriya tickets in advance?

No. You can pay online at the Central Cultural Fund’s site (eservices.ccf.gov.lk), but you still exchange the receipt at the counter, so it saves the payment, not the queue. Tickets are priced in dollars and paid in rupees at the day’s rate; bring your passport if you’re claiming a SAARC or child ticket.

Do I need a guide?

No — the path is one way up and one way down, and the museum tells the story. A licensed guide from the office by the car park adds a lot on the frescoes and the water gardens; expect around Rs 3,000 for two hours, plus a tip.

Where do people go after Sigiriya?

Most go south to Kandy and then the hill country. If you want beaches in the European summer, the east coast is the one in season — Trincomalee is about 2–2 h 30 from here. See the routes ↓

Which climb should I do?

If you only do one, make it Sigiriya — it’s the one people build the trip around. With two days, add Pidurangala for the view back at the fortress. Dambulla is the easiest of the three and pairs well with either.

How hard is Sigiriya?

Moderate: about 1,200 steps, some narrow metal staircases bolted to the rock near the top, and the heat does the rest. Most people reach the summit in 45 to 75 minutes. Go at opening time; the metal stairs are uncomfortable to touch by late morning.

Is Pidurangala harder than Sigiriya?

Shorter but rougher. Most people are up in 30 to 45 minutes, but the path is less built-up and the last part is a scramble over boulders. It’s the one for sunrise, and the one with the famous view of Sigiriya itself.

Is Dambulla a hard climb?

No — paved steps and a steady uphill walk, 20 to 30 minutes to the caves. It’s hot and exposed, so go in the late afternoon, after an early climb up Sigiriya.

Is it OK for children or anyone scared of heights?

Children who are fine on stairs love Sigiriya, but the last section is steep, open metal stairs — hold hands. Pidurangala’s final scramble isn’t for small children or anyone nervous of heights. Dambulla is fine for everyone.

What should I wear?
  • Light, breathable clothes
  • Shoulders and knees covered for temples
  • Closed shoes with grip
  • A hat and sunglasses
What should I bring?

At least a litre of water each, sunscreen, insect repellent, socks for the hot temple floors, and cash. No strong perfume on the climb — the hornets notice.

What if I get ill or hurt here?

There are pharmacies in Dambulla and Kimbissa and a hospital in Dambulla. Heat is what catches people out — climb early, drink more than you think and rest in the middle of the day.

  • Carry cash. Pidurangala, the Dambulla caves, the cafés and the jeep drivers are cash-only, and ATMs in Sigiriya village are hard to find — take out rupees in Dambulla or before you arrive.
  • Elephants walk the roads around Sigiriya, especially at dawn, dusk and after dark. Don’t walk or cycle the village roads then — take a tuk-tuk.
  • Your Sigiriya ticket is single entry: once you’re out, you’re out. Do the museum on the way out, not before.
  • Hornets nest near the Lion’s Paw. Keep your voice down there, skip the perfume, and follow the staff if they stop the climb.
  • No photographs of the Sigiriya frescoes. And at any temple, never pose with your back to a Buddha statue.
  • Drones need written permission at Sigiriya — don’t bring one out.
  • At the Dambulla caves, buy the ticket at the office by the car park before you climb — there’s no booth at the top.
  • Spice gardens are lovely, but the oils and remedies at the end are expensive. You never have to buy.
  • Tuk-tuk fares aren’t metered. Agree the price first; your hotel will tell you what’s fair today.
  • Full-moon (Poya) days: alcohol isn’t sold and the temples are busy with pilgrims. Check the date before you plan a big dinner.
