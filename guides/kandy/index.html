Kandy

Sri Lanka’s last royal capital, wrapped around a lake 500 m up in the hills. The Temple of the Tooth stands on its shore, and the whole city has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988.

Thinking about the train?

Right now no train reaches Kandy. Cyclone Ditwah cut the main line in late 2025, and trains from Colombo currently turn back at Rambukkana, well short of the hills. The railway says Colombo–Kandy trains are expected back in October 2026, but there’s no timetable yet and a bridge just outside Kandy is still being repaired — so check before you plan around one. Kandy → Nanu Oya, the start of the famous ride to Ella, is still suspended.

Until it’s back, we drive it: about 3 h from the airport, 3 h 45 to Ella. When the line reopens, sit on the right out of Colombo, and on the right from Kandy as far as Nanu Oya. Reserved seats go on sale 30 days ahead, only on seatreservation.railway.gov.lk. Message us and we’ll tell you what’s running on your date.

Things to see & do

Eight things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic

Sri Dalada Maligawa keeps Sri Lanka’s most precious relic, a tooth of the Buddha, in a golden casket behind the doors of an inner chamber. It’s a working temple first: pilgrims dressed in white, and drums at the services. One ticket also covers three museums and the royal elephant stable in the same grounds.

From townIn the centre, on the lake’s north shore
Open5:30 AM–8 PM daily · services at 5:30, 9:30 & 6:30 PM
Give it1–1.5 hours
EffortEasy
TicketRs 3,000 non-SAARC · Rs 2,500 SAARC · from the machines only
From our driversCome for the 6:30 PM service: drums, oil lamps, and the chamber doors open — you see the golden casket, not the tooth itself. Go at 5:30 AM if you want it quiet. Buy only from the ticket machines; anyone selling a ‘special ticket’ to see the relic is running a scam. The audio guide is free, in 8 languages.

2

Kandy Lake & the viewpoint

Kiri Muhuda, the ‘sea of milk’, was made in 1807 by King Sri Wickrama Rajasinghe, right beside the temple. A 3.4 km path runs all the way round it under the trees. Above it on Rajapihilla Mawatha, Arthur’s Seat — the Kandy View Point — gives you the postcard: lake, temple and city in one frame, with the hills behind.

From townThe centre of town
OpenLake path always open · no ticket
Give it1 hour round the lake · 20 min at the viewpoint
EffortEasy
From our driversWalk the loop at about four, then take a tuk-tuk up to the viewpoint for the late light, in time for a 5 PM dance show. Painting and souvenir sellers work the rail at the top — a smile and a no is fine.
3

Royal Botanic Gardens, Peradeniya

Sri Lanka’s royal botanic gardens, a short drive out of town: avenues of palms, old trees and enough paths to fill a whole morning. The walking is flat, but the grounds are big — this is the one place in Kandy to give real time.

From townA short drive out of town
Open7:30 AM–6 PM, every day · last tickets about 5 PM
Give it2–3 hours
EffortEasy · flat, but big
Ticket≈ Rs 3,540 foreign adult · ≈ Rs 1,770 child
From our driversBe there at 7:30 when the gates open, before the heat. Bring water and a hat — the grounds are huge. If your legs give out, electric buggies can be hired inside. There’s no working online booking; pay at the gate.
4

Bahirawakanda Buddha

The big white Buddha you can see from all over town, seated in meditation on the hill above the city, about 27 m tall. It’s a short, steep climb from the centre, and the terrace looks out over the rooftops, the lake and the hills beyond.

From townAbout 2 km · a steep walk or a short tuk-tuk
OpenAbout 6 AM–6 PM
Give it45–60 min
EffortModerate · short and steep
Ticket≈ Rs 300 · cash
Good to knowIt’s a working temple: shoes off at the kiosk, shoulders and knees covered. The lane up is steep and exposed — take a tuk-tuk in the midday heat and walk down.
5

Udawattakele Forest Reserve

A hill of forest right behind the Temple of the Tooth: shady trails, birds and monkeys, a quiet escape minutes from the busiest streets in town. Few open views — it’s about the trees and the quiet.

From townBehind the temple, about 1 km
Open8 AM–6 PM · tickets until 4 PM
Give it1.5–2 hours
EffortModerate · hilly trails
Ticket≈ Rs 570–930 · check at the gate
Good to knowLeeches in the wet months — long socks or leech socks help. Keep food out of sight of the monkeys. Keen birders can arrange an early entry from 6 AM; a guide is about Rs 500 extra.
6

A Kandyan dance show

An hour of drums, spinning, leaping dancers in the Kandyan costume and, at the end, fire-walking. Several halls in town put on a show at about five: the Kandy Lake Club, the YMBA and the Red Cross hall.

From townHalls in the centre
OpenAbout 5–6 PM
Give it1 hour
EffortEasy
Ticket≈ USD 20 reserved at the Lake Club · ≈ Rs 2,000 at the YMBA or the Red Cross hall
From our driversThe 5 PM show finishes in time to walk to the 6:30 service at the temple — the best evening in Kandy. Get there 10–15 minutes early for a good seat.
7

Ceylon Tea Museum

An old tea factory in the Hantane hills, turned into four floors of machinery, history and, at the end, tea. Finish in the top-floor tea café, where a telescope looks out to the Knuckles and Hunnasgiriya ranges.

From town3 km · in the Hantane hills
OpenTue–Fri 8:30 AM–4 PM · Sat–Sun 8 AM–5 PM · closed Mondays
Give it1–1.5 hours
EffortEasy
TicketRs 1,000 adult · Rs 500 child
From our driversClosed on Mondays, but open on Poya days and public holidays — handy when everything else is quiet. Want more tea? Ask us about a walk or a guided ride through the estates in the Hanthana hills, just above it.
8

Ambuluwawa Tower

A white spiral tower on a forested hilltop near Gampola, with a 360° view over the hill country. The last flights of stairs wind round the outside of the spire, narrow and open to the drop — the climb is half the point.

From townAbout 20 km · about 1 h by car, near Gampola
OpenAbout 8 AM–5:30 PM
Give it1–1.5 hours, plus the drive
EffortModerate · hard if you dislike heights
Ticket≈ Rs 2,000–2,750 · sources differ, check at the gate
From our driversGo early, before the heat and the cloud. You can stop at any level — the top stairs are narrow and exposed, so skip the last bit if heights bother you. It’s just off the road to Nuwara Eliya and Ella, so we can take you up on the way out of Kandy — just ask.
Further afield

Worth a whole day, if you have one

≈ 45 mineach wayThe three temples loop

Embekke, Lankatilaka and Gadaladeniya, three 14th-century temples past the rice fields beyond Peradeniya. Embekke is famous for its carved wooden pillars. An easy half day by car — or walk the 7 km path between them. Small fees at each, cash.

≈ 2 heach wayKitulgala rafting

White water on the Kelani River — grade 2–3 rapids, with water all year. Better as a stop on the drive to or from Colombo than a day trip there and back. Tell us and we’ll build it in, and check the operators are running.

≈ 3 heach wayThe Knuckles Range

Cloud forest, villages, waterfalls and very few people. Tour operators reckon about three hours to the trails, so it’s a full day, best with a local guide — message us and we’ll help you plan it.

When to come

The weather, month by month

Kandy is warm all year, 27–30° by day, and barely changes. Rain is what matters: March is the driest month and November the wettest.

Jan – AprDry & clear

The driest, clearest months — March most of all — and peak season. Mid-April is Sinhala & Tamil New Year, one of the busiest weeks for hotels.

Best weather
May – JulSome showers

Rain comes and goes with the south-west monsoon. Quieter than the dry months, and very green.

AugPerahera month

The Esala Perahera usually falls in July or August, on dates the temple sets by the lunar calendar — 2026’s ran 18–28 August; 2027’s aren’t announced yet. Book rooms early.

Festival
Sep – DecThe rains

The wettest stretch of the year, peaking in October and November. Take a rain jacket, and expect leeches on the forest trails.

Where to stay

Stay

Stay in the centre or by the lake and you can walk to everything; the hill hotels have the views, but you’ll need a tuk-tuk. Book well ahead for August.

Luxury

The Kandy House · Santani

The Kandy House is an 1804 manor outside town, eight rooms and a suite around an infinity pool, with a Michelin listing (over-12s only). Santani, 14 km out in the hills, is a proper Ayurveda retreat — a doctor’s consultation and programmes of 5 to 21 days. Both are a drive from the centre; we’ll get you in and out.

Queen’s Hotel · Ru Boutique · Amaya Hills

Queen’s Hotel is the colonial landmark by the lake and the temple, with a pool, and you walk to everything. Ru Boutique has 14 rooms right on the lakefront. Amaya Hills, near Peradeniya, is a traditional hotel with mountain views and its own Ayurvedic spa. In town, ask for a room facing the lake.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

How many nights should I stay?

One or two. One full day covers the gardens, the lake, a dance show and the evening service at the temple. A second lets you add the Tea Museum, a forest walk or Ambuluwawa without rushing.

Is there a train to Kandy?

Not right now. Cyclone Ditwah cut the line in late 2025. Colombo–Kandy trains are expected back from October 2026, but nothing is timetabled yet and Kandy → Nanu Oya is still suspended — so check before you plan. We drive it: about 3 h from the airport, 3 h 45 to Ella. See how we get you here ↑

When is the Esala Perahera?

Usually in July or August. The temple sets the dates each year by the lunar calendar and auspicious times; 2026’s ran 18–28 August, and 2027’s haven’t been announced yet. The centre is reported to close to traffic on procession evenings, so plan to reach your hotel by mid-afternoon — and book your room early.

Which viewpoint or short hike is for me?

Short on time: Arthur’s Seat, a tuk-tuk ride up for the lake-and-temple view. The landmark: the Bahirawakanda Buddha. Shade and birds: Udawattakele. A real hike: the Hanthana hills — trails of one to three hours through tea. The thrill: Ambuluwawa’s spiral tower, an hour away. See them ↑

Is there any nightlife?

A little, and it’s low-key. Slightly Chilled Lounge has a rooftop, pool tables and fruit bats over the terrace at dusk; The Pub on Dalada Veediya has sport on the screens; Tavern 29 does cocktails on a rooftop. Alcohol isn’t sold on full-moon (Poya) days.

Where do people go after Kandy?

Most go up into the tea country — Nuwara Eliya is 2 h 45, Ella 3 h 45 — or north to Sigiriya, 2 h 30, if they haven’t been yet. At the end of a trip, the airport is about 3 h. From roughly April to September, Trincomalee on the east coast is the beach that’s in season, about 4 h away. See the routes ↓

What should I wear?

Shoulders and knees covered — shorts, sleeveless tops and anything revealing aren’t allowed in, though you can get suitable clothes at the entrance. White is traditional but not required. Shoes go into the foreigners’ booths for a token, so bring socks; the stone gets hot.

Can I see the tooth?

No. During the services the doors of the relic chamber open and you see the golden casket that holds it — that’s as close as anyone gets.

When is the best time to go?

The 6:30 PM service is the most atmospheric — drums, oil lamps, the chamber doors open — and the busiest. 5:30 AM is the quietest; 9:30 AM is in between. The temple is open 5:30 AM–8 PM every day.

How much is it, and where do I buy tickets?

Rs 3,000 if you’re from outside the SAARC countries; Rs 2,500 from SAARC countries, Thailand and Myanmar. Tickets come only from the temple’s own machines, and cover the temple, three museums and the royal elephant stable. There’s no online booking, and tickets sold by anyone else aren’t valid. No child rate is published. Bring cash in case the machines don’t take your card.

Do I need a guide?

No. Foreign visitors get a free audio guide in 8 languages. Skip the touts at the gate.

Anything for the gardens and the forest?

Water and a hat for Peradeniya — there’s a lot of ground to cover. Proper shoes for Udawattakele, and long socks or leech socks there in the wet months.

  • Carry cash in small notes. Temple tickets come from machines, and the smaller sights, dance halls and some cafés are cash only. Bank of Ceylon and People’s Bank ATMs are reported not to charge foreign cards; when the machine asks, choose rupees, not your home currency.
  • PickMe and Uber both work in Kandy, and PickMe shows the fare upfront. For a tuk-tuk off the street, agree the price first.
  • At the Temple of the Tooth, buy only from the ticket machines. ‘Special tickets’ to see the relic are a scam.
  • If anyone offers a ‘free gem museum’ visit, it ends in a showroom. We’ll only stop if you ask.
  • Spice gardens on the Sigiriya road are lovely, but the oils and remedies at the end are a hard sell. You never have to buy.
  • The Ceylon Tea Museum is closed on Mondays — and open on Poya days and public holidays.
  • Poya (full-moon) days are dry: bars and shops stop selling alcohol. Some big hotels may still serve their own guests. Check the date before you plan a night out.
  • Busiest times: the Perahera (usually August), Sinhala & Tamil New Year in mid-April, and Vesak. Book your room and your car early.
  • The climb at Kadugannawa and the road to Nuwara Eliya both twist. If you get car-sick, sit up front, eat light and tell your driver — we’ll stop.
  • Ambuluwawa’s top stairs are narrow and exposed. Skip the last bit if you don’t like heights.
