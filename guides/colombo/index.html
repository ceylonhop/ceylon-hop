Thinking about the train?

The coastal line between Colombo and Galle is running normally. It’s the one worth taking: sit on the right going south for the sea, and on the left coming back. Reserved seats are sold only on seatreservation.railway.gov.lk — you need every passenger’s passport number when you book, and the original passport on the train.

The Kandy line still isn’t running through. Since Cyclone Ditwah, trains from Colombo go only as far as Rambukkana. The railway expects to reopen it in stages, but a bridge just outside Kandy is still being repaired, so check before you plan. Message us and we’ll check for your date — and drive you if it’s not running.

Things to see & do

Seven things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Pettah market & the Red Mosque

Colombo at full volume: crowded market lanes next to the Fort, carts and horns, fabric, shoes and gadgets piled high. In the middle of it stands Jami Ul-Alfar, the Red Mosque, striped red and white like a stick of rock — free to visit, with a guide from the mosque to show you round.

From townNext to the Fort
OpenMarket about 9 AM–7 PM · mosque from 9:30 AM
Give it2 hours
EffortModerate · hot, crowded, on foot
TicketFree · the mosque guide is free too
Good to knowGo before ten, ahead of the heat and the crush. It’s mostly cash, keep your phone and wallet in front of you, and treat every famous brand as a copy. At the mosque, use gate 3 and dress modestly. On Fridays visitors are let in only in the afternoon — times change, so check the day before.
2

Colombo Fort & the Old Dutch Hospital

The old colonial heart, now banks and office towers with the old buildings still standing between them: the Cargills building, the Grand Oriental Hotel and Wolvendaal Church nearby. The Old Dutch Hospital, thought to be the oldest building in the Fort, is now courtyards of shops and cafés, a big Spa Ceylon and Ministry of Crab.

From townCentral · a short walk from Pettah
OpenShops and cafés keep their own hours
Give it1–1.5 hours on foot
EffortEasy
TicketFree
From our driversWalk it straight after Pettah, then stop for a coffee in the Dutch Hospital’s courtyards before the midday heat. If you want the history to make sense, this is where a walking guide earns the fee.
3

National Museum & Independence Square

Sri Lanka’s national collection, in Cinnamon Gardens — the leafy side of the city — next to Viharamahadevi Park, Colombo’s oldest and largest. A short ride away, Independence Memorial Hall marks the day Sri Lanka became independent, 4 February 1948.

From townCinnamon Gardens · 10–15 min from the Fort
Open9 AM–6 PM · last tickets 5 PM
Give it1.5–2 hours
EffortEasy
Ticket≈ Rs 1,200 foreign adult · check at the gate
Good to knowThe museum closes on public holidays, including every full-moon Poya day — check the date. Reviews are mixed on value: go if you love history, skip it if you’d rather be outside.
4

Gangaramaya & Seema Malakaya

Gangaramaya is a busy working temple by Beira Lake, with a museum and row upon row of Buddha statues. Seema Malakaya, a short walk away, is the calm one: Geoffrey Bawa’s 1979 pavilion that seems to float on the lake.

From townBeira Lake, central
OpenAbout 6 AM–10 PM · Seema Malakaya to 9 PM
Give itAbout 1.5 hours
EffortEasy
Ticket≈ Rs 500, one ticket for both · cash
From our driversCover shoulders and knees, shoes off, and never pose with your back to a Buddha — it’s taken seriously here. If a friendly stranger tells you there’s a special festival today and waves down a tuk-tuk, smile and walk on: that ride ends at a gem shop.
5

Galle Face Green at sunset

A long stretch of grass between Galle Road and the sea, Colombo’s seafront playground since 1859. Come at about five: kites go up, the food carts fire up, and families stroll the promenade as the sun drops into the Indian Ocean. Part of the new Port City promenade next door is open too.

From townColombo 3 · by the Galle Face Hotel
OpenAlways open · food stalls from about 5 PM
Give it1 hour at sunset
EffortEasy
TicketFree
From our driversEat isso vadai — a fried patty topped with prawns — from the cart with the longest local queue. Then walk over to the Galle Face Hotel next door, where a bagpiper lowers the flag at sunset.
6

Lotus Tower

The pink-and-green spire you can see from all over the city, with an observation deck about 240 m above sea level. Go after dark, when Colombo lights up below you and the tower glows. Twenty minutes at the top is plenty for most people.

From townNear Beira Lake, Colombo 10
OpenTickets 9 AM–9 PM · weekends to 10 PM
Give it30–45 min
EffortEasy · there’s a lift
TicketUSD 20 adult · USD 10 age 3–10 · paid in rupees
Good to knowBook a time slot on the official site, tickets.colombolotustower.lk, up to three months ahead — tickets can’t be refunded or transferred. Bring your passport: it’s checked at the door. An evening slot is the one worth paying for.
7

A street-food tour by tuk-tuk

The best way to spend a single evening here: about three hours, hopping between hoppers, kottu, isso vadai and a king coconut, from Pettah’s lanes to tea on Galle Face Green. Several local operators run them, and it doubles as dinner.

Further afield

Worth a whole day, if you have one

30–60 mineach wayNegombo

The beach town by the airport: a fish market where the catch dries on the sand, St Mary’s Church and the old Dutch canal. Handy for your last night before an early flight.

30–45 mineach wayKelaniya Raja Maha Vihara

A big working temple about 11 km north-east of the centre, with Solias Mendis’s murals of the Buddha’s past lives inside. No foreigner ticket — a donation is welcome. Shoulders and knees covered.

1 h 30each wayBentota & the Madu River

A mangrove river of 64 islands, explored by boat in about an hour, with Bevis Bawa’s Brief Garden and Geoffrey Bawa’s Lunuganga close by. Best as a stop on the way to Galle, not a day trip there and back.

When to come

The weather, month by month

Colombo is on the west coast, so it gets the south-west monsoon: driest from December to March, wettest in October and November.

Dec – MarDry & busy

The driest months — January and February see rain on only about five days each. Peak season: book hotels and Ministry of Crab well ahead.

Best time
Apr – JunHot, then the monsoon

Hot and sticky, with the first monsoon rains peaking in May. Mid-April is Sinhala & Tamil New Year; Vesak falls at the May full moon.

Jul – AugA drier lull

The rain eases to well under half of May’s — still showers, but plenty of dry days. The Vel festival procession usually runs down Galle Road around now.

Sep – NovThe wettest

October and November are the wettest months, with rain on more than half the days in October. Fewer visitors; Deepavali falls in October or November.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

Is Colombo worth a day?

Yes — one. Most people land, sleep and leave, but one well-planned day is enough for Pettah, the Fort, a temple by the lake and sunset on Galle Face Green. Stay a second night only for a day trip, or to rest after a long flight.

First or last night: Colombo or Negombo?

For a flight before about 8 AM, sleep in Negombo or by the airport — no city traffic between you and the terminal. With a day to spare, stay in Colombo and see it: the airport is about 50 minutes away, longer at rush hour.

Which area should I stay in?

Colombo 3 and 7 (Kollupitiya, Cinnamon Gardens): the seafront, the best restaurants and most of the big hotels. The Fort: the old buildings and the station, busy by day and quiet at night. Mount Lavinia: a beach suburb to the south, sleepier and further from everything. Negombo: your last night before an early flight.

Do I need a guide?

In Pettah and the Fort, a guide earns the fee: the lanes start to make sense and the touts leave you alone. Walks run from tip-based to about USD 128 for a four-hour premium walk. Galle Face, the temples and the tower are easy on your own.

What closes on Poya days?

Every full moon is a public holiday. Expect a dry day: bars and shops stop selling alcohol, though some hotels serve their own guests. The National Museum and Ministry of Crab close. Check the date before you plan a big dinner.

Where do people go after Colombo?

South to Galle — about 2 h 15 down the expressway — or inland to Kandy, about 3 h 30. From May to September, the east coast is the beach in season. See the routes ↓

How long is the drive to the airport?

About 50 minutes from the city on the expressway, and an hour or more at rush hour. Aim to be at the terminal about three hours before an international flight — tell us your flight time and we’ll set the pickup.

Can I check in early?

Bandaranaike International has started letting passengers check in up to four hours before departure, and has self-service kiosks and bag drop. Useful if your hotel checkout is at noon and your flight is in the evening.

Where do I get a SIM card?

At the mobile-network counters in the arrivals hall. Sort it before you leave the terminal, so the app and the maps work from the start.

Can I use ride-hailing apps?

PickMe is the main app, for cars and tuk-tuks; Uber works mostly in Colombo. At the airport, check the plate and the driver match the app before you get in. Or book with us, and your driver meets you at arrivals.

Is there a bus from the airport?

Yes — route 187, an air-conditioned bus along the expressway to the Colombo Fort bus stand. It leaves from the outer porch of the departure terminal.

What should I wear?
  • Loose cotton or linen
  • Shoulders and knees covered for temples
  • Shoes that slip off easily
  • A compact umbrella (Apr–Jun, Sep–Nov)
What should I bring?

Your passport for Lotus Tower and any reserved train. A sarong or shawl — Pettah and Barefoot sell them — doubles as temple cover. Sunscreen, a hat, a refillable bottle, and rupees for Pettah and the temples.

Is it a problem to have a Buddha tattoo?

It can be. Visible Buddha tattoos can get you refused entry to temples, or even deported. Keep them covered.

  • The festival scam: a friendly, respectable-looking stranger says there’s a special temple festival today and flags you a tuk-tuk. There’s no festival — the ride ends at a gem shop. Smile and walk on.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or hotels — card fraud is a known problem. Bag-snatching from motorbikes happens too, so keep your bag on the side away from the road.
  • Carry cash for Pettah, the street food and the temples. Lotus Tower charges your card in rupees; the National Museum takes cards but prefers cash.
  • Tuk-tuks: book through PickMe or Uber so the fare is set, or agree the price before you get in.
  • Poya (full-moon) days: expect a dry day — some hotels serve their own guests. The National Museum and Ministry of Crab are closed.
  • Never pose with your back to a Buddha statue, at Gangaramaya or anywhere else.
  • Book Ministry of Crab ahead, especially December to March, and be on time: tables aren’t held after 15 minutes.
  • Mount Lavinia’s beach is for sunsets, not swimming: rip currents have pulled swimmers out, and lifeguards only work at busy times.
  • Drink-spiking and harassment in crowded places are both reported. Watch your drink at night, and in the Pettah crowds keep your wits about you.
  • Rush hour turns the 50-minute airport run into an hour or more. Tell us your flight time and we’ll plan the pickup around it.
