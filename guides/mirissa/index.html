Mirissa

A crescent of sand on the south coast, ringed by palms, with a fishing harbour at one end where the whale boats leave at dawn. Turtles in the shallows, a palm-topped headland for sunset, and a much slower pace after the hill country.

Thinking about the train?

Mirissa has its own station, but few trains stop there. A train or two a day from Colombo Fort calls at Mirissa; more stop at Weligama, 10–15 minutes away. Allow 3–4 hours. The coastal line has had derailments and closures in the past year — November 2025 and April 2026 — so check before you rely on it. We drive it door to door, and the expressway is quicker.

Things to see & do

Six things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Whale watching

Mirissa is one of the best places in the world to see blue whales: the continental shelf drops away a few kilometres offshore, and from November to April the boats find blue whales, sperm whales and spinner dolphins on most mornings. It’s a long morning at sea — and the reason many people come.

From townMirissa Harbour · 5 min
OpenNov–Apr · boats leave about 6:00–6:30
Give it4–5 hours, sometimes longer
EffortEasy · hard if you get seasick
Ticket≈ USD 40–80 on a shared boat
Good to knowSightings are likely in season but never guaranteed. Choose an operator that keeps its distance and doesn’t chase the whales — the law says at least 100 m, engines off inside that. Take a seasickness tablet before you board, not after.
2

Coconut Tree Hill

A red-earth headland at the west end of the bay, covered in tall palms leaning out over the sea — the photo of Mirissa you’ve probably already seen. Go at sunrise for soft light and an empty hill, or at sunset with everyone else.

From townWest end of the beach · 15 min walk
OpenSunrise or sunset
Give it45 min
TicketFree
Good to knowIt’s private land that the owners leave open to visitors — they briefly fenced it off in 2021. Stay on the paths and keep back from the cliff edge.
3

Parrot Rock & Secret Beach

Parrot Rock is the little islet just off the main beach, with steps up to a lookout over the whole bay — a good place for the sun going down. Secret Beach is a quieter cove about 2 km west, past the headland: a tuk-tuk up a steep lane, then a 10-minute walk down.

From townParrot Rock: on the beach · Secret Beach: 10 min by tuk-tuk
OpenParrot Rock at low tide only
Give it1–2 hours
TicketFree
Good to knowOnly wade out to Parrot Rock at low tide. At high tide the water is waist-deep and the current pulls, especially from May to October — and the steps on the rock are uneven.
4

Turtle Point

At the foot of Coconut Tree Hill, green and hawksbill turtles graze on the algae in water you can stand in. Bring a mask, float quietly, and they’ll swim right past you.

From townWest end of the beach · 15 min walk
OpenCalm mornings
Give it1 hour
EffortEasy · shallow water
TicketFree · bring or hire a mask
Good to knowDon’t feed or touch the turtles, however many people around you do — sellers on the beach offer seaweed for it. Watch from a few metres away.
5

Mirissa Harbour at dawn

Before the whale boats go out, the night’s fishing boats come in. From first light the catch is landed and auctioned on the quay, and it’s all over within the hour — the working side of Mirissa most visitors sleep through.

From townEast end of the bay · 5 min
OpenFrom first light, about 6:00
Give it30–45 min
TicketFree
From our driversPair it with the whale boat — they leave from the same harbour at the same hour. Keep out of the way of the crews unloading.
6

Kayaking the Garanduwa lagoon

Just inland of the beach, the Garanduwa lagoon winds through the mangroves, and a slow paddle along its channels is the quietest two hours you’ll spend here. Look for kingfishers, herons and water monitors on the banks.

From townJust inland · about 10 min by tuk-tuk
OpenEarly morning
Give itAbout 2 hours
EffortEasy · fine for first-timers
TicketVaries by operator · pickup usually included
From our driversGo early, when the water is still and the birds are out. Most tours collect you from your guesthouse.
Further afield

Worth a whole day, if you have one

45 min–1 heach wayBlue Beach Island, Nilwella

A small island off Nilwella beach that you walk out to across a sandbar at low tide — check the tide times first. You can book a night’s camping on the island locally; book well ahead. Hiriketiya is next door.

10–15 mineach wayWeligama

The long, gentle bay next door is where Sri Lanka learns to surf: soft beach-break waves and surf schools all along the sand. Better than Mirissa if you’ve never stood on a board.

2 h 30+each wayUdawalawe or Yala

Both parks are possible in a day, but it means a 4 AM start and five or six hours in the car. Better to spend a night near the park on your way — tell us your dates and we’ll plan the drive.

When to come

The weather, month by month

Warm all year, 29–31° by day. What changes is the sea: calm from December to March, rough in the south-west monsoon from May to September — and October and November are the wettest months of all.

Dec – MarPeak season

Calm seas, the best odds for whales and the driest months, around 90–110 mm of rain. The busiest time to come — book your room early.

Best for whales
Apr & NovShoulder months

The whale boats run, but sightings are less reliable than in peak season. Showers build from mid-April, and November is one of the wettest months.

May – SepSouth-west monsoon

Rougher seas, heavy showers and red flags on the beach on many days, and the whale boats mostly stop. Quieter and cheaper — the east coast is in season instead.

OctThe wettest month

About 300 mm of rain, the most of the year on this coast. Hotels are quiet until the season starts again in November.

After dark

Nightlife

Mirissa’s own beach is mellow: sunset drinks and music. For a proper night out, the big parties are 15 minutes east in Madiha, on Wednesdays and Saturdays.

Beach bars

Along Mirissa beach

At dusk the bars along the beach put out cushions and lanterns: a cold beer or a cocktail as the sun goes down behind the headland, then music into the night. Walk the sand and pick the crowd you like.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

How many nights should I stay?

Two or three: a morning on the whale boat, a morning for the lagoon or the turtles, and a sunset or two on the headlands. Stay longer if you want to learn to surf in Weligama, next door.

When is whale season?

November to April, with the best odds from December to March, when the sea is calmest. From May to October the boats mostly stop. See the months ↓

Is Mirissa good for families?

Yes. The main beach is calm in season and Turtle Point is shallow. The whale boat is a long, rolling morning — better for older children than toddlers — and the steps on Parrot Rock are uneven. Keep little ones close in the water: there are rip currents near the headlands.

What else is there to do?

Night snorkelling with a guide (about USD 55–61 for 2 hours, with gear and hotel pickup), a Sri Lankan cooking class (about USD 15–30), stand-up paddleboarding, and surf lessons in Weligama, 10–15 minutes away.

Can I visit Yala or Udawalawe as a day trip?

You can, but it’s a 4 AM start and five or six hours in the car for a few hours in the park. Better to spend a night near the park on your way — Yala is about 3 hours from here. See the routes ↓

What’s the easiest way to get to Mirissa?

By car, door to door. From the airport it’s about 2 h 45 on the expressway, from Ella about 3 hours. If you take the coast road from Galle instead, you pass the stilt fishermen at Koggala and Ahangama — we’ll stop wherever you like.

Can I take the train?

Yes, but only a train or two a day from Colombo Fort stops at Mirissa itself; more stop at Weligama, 10–15 minutes away by tuk-tuk. Allow 3–4 hours. The coastal line was disrupted by derailments in November 2025 and April 2026, so check the timetable before you go.

What about the bus?

The expressway buses from Colombo (Bastian Mawatha or Makumbura) reach Matara in about 2.5 hours; from there it’s a short tuk-tuk back to Mirissa. Ordinary buses on the Galle Road stop in Mirissa itself, but they’re slow and get very full.

What should I wear in town?

Swimwear on the beach; a shirt and shorts or a sarong when you walk into town. Topless sunbathing isn’t allowed anywhere in Sri Lanka.

  • Swim near the lifeguard post and follow the flags. Rip currents run near the headlands, the sea is rough from May to October, and the Coast Guard has had to rescue swimmers here.
  • Whale sightings are likely in season but never guaranteed. A good operator keeps 100 m from the whales and doesn’t chase them.
  • Don’t feed or touch the turtles at Turtle Point.
  • Cross to Parrot Rock at low tide only.
  • Mirissa has surf — a reef break at the west end for intermediates, and a small section for beginners — but Weligama, 10–15 minutes away, is the better place to learn.
  • Coconut Tree Hill is private land left open to visitors. Be respectful, and keep back from the edge.
  • Poya (full-moon) days are dry: bars and shops stop selling alcohol, and the parties may not happen.
  • December to March is busy. Book your room early.
