Arugam Bay

A surf village on the east coast, just south of Pottuvil, whose right-hand points come alive just as the south coast’s season ends. Between sessions there are crocodiles in the lagoon, elephants by the road and a jungle hermitage on a rock.

Things to see & do

Seven things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Main Point & Baby Point

The reason people come: a long right-hand point break right in town, with rides of 50–150 m and 400 m or more on the best days, at its best on a south-easterly swell. Baby Point is its inside section — soft, slow waves where the surf schools take first-timers. Not surfing? Watch from the sand at dawn.

From townIn town
OpenApr–Sep · best at dawn
Give it2 hours a session
EffortBaby: beginner · Main: intermediate–advanced
TicketFree · lessons ≈ USD 20–40
From our driversFirst time? Stay on Baby Point and leave Main to the confident surfers — it breaks over a reef shelf and it’s crowded, especially June to August. Paddle out at dawn, before the wind gets up.
2

Whiskey Point

A right-hander 10–15 minutes north of town towards Pottuvil, and the most consistent wave in the area: punchy but forgiving, with rides of 100–200 m. The step up once you can catch a wave and turn — and a quieter stretch of beach to stay on.

From town10–15 min north by tuk-tuk
OpenApr–Sep · mornings
Give itA morning
EffortIntermediate
TicketFree
Good to knowRocks and boulders on the inside — reef booties help. Friday night is party night at Sababa here, so it’s less quiet at the end of the week.
3

Elephant Rock & Peanut Farm

South of town off the Panama road, two gentler waves: Peanut Farm, about 20 minutes away at the end of a long beach (stick to the inside peak — the outside is steep and rocky), and Elephant Rock, a fat, soft wave that loves longboards. Elephant Rock is also the bay’s sunset spot, a short walk from the road.

From town15–20 min south by tuk-tuk, then a short walk
OpenMornings to surf · back for sunset
Give itA morning, or an hour at sunset
EffortBeginner–lower intermediate
TicketFree
Good to knowCrocodiles live in the lagoon behind Elephant Rock, and someone was killed there in 2017. Surf and swim on the sea side only, and keep away from the lagoon edge. Wild elephants are reported around Peanut Farm too.
4

Pottuvil Lagoon mangrove safari

A slow trip by boat or canoe through the mangrove channels of the lagoon at Pottuvil, looking for crocodiles, water birds and, sometimes, elephants at the water’s edge. The calmest two hours you’ll spend here.

From townPottuvil · about 10 min
OpenDawn or late afternoon
Give it2–3 hours
EffortEasy
Ticket≈ USD 23–35 per adult, by operator
From our driversGo early or late — the wildlife is most active then, and the middle of the day is too hot. Any of the tour desks on the main road can book it the day before.
5

Kumana National Park

The eastern side of Yala, entered at Okanda down the Panama road: lagoons, scrub jungle and the Kumana Villu wetland. You may see elephants, crocodiles and, sometimes, a leopard, but it’s the birds people come for — the migrants peak from April to July.

From townOkanda gate · 1 h+ down the Panama road
Open6 AM–6 PM · by jeep
Give itHalf a day
EffortEasy · by jeep
Ticketabout USD 25–27 plus charges and VAT (2026) — check at the gate
Good to knowBring cash — there’s no online entry ticket, and cards at the gate aren’t certain. Hire a jeep in the bay (about Rs 7,000–10,000 for a half day) or book a tour that bundles jeep and tickets. The park can close after heavy rain, as it did in late 2025.
6

Kudumbigala Forest Hermitage

A forest hermitage in the jungle south of Panama: cave temples scattered among the boulders, said to date back to 246 BC, and a climb up bare rock at the top for a view over the treetops. A quiet, unhurried place.

From townAbout 30 km · 45–60 min south
Give itAbout 2 hours
EffortModerate · a rock climb at the top
From our driversPair it with Kumana on one run south — they’re down the same road. Cover shoulders and knees, take your shoes off at the shrines, and wear shoes with grip for the rock.

Muhudu Maha Viharaya

An ancient temple on the dunes of Pottuvil beach, over 2,000 years old and tied to the legend of the princess Viharamaha Devi, who is said to have washed ashore here. Half an hour, with the sea right behind the ruins.

When to come

The weather, month by month

The east coast runs the opposite way to the south: dry with surf from about April to September, then the north-east monsoon brings the rain from November to March, when many places close.

Apr – MaySeason opens

The swell arrives and the rains are over — May averages under 50 mm. Fewer people than mid-year: a good time to learn.

Quieter start
Jun – AugPeak season

The driest months (under 20 mm of rain a month) and the hottest, up to 35°. Main Point and Peanut Farm are at their busiest and the beach parties run. Book your room early.

Best for surf
Sep – OctShoulder

Still surfable, emptier and cheaper. September averages about 52 mm of rain; by the end of October the season is winding down.

Nov – MarClosed season

The north-east monsoon: January alone averages about 263 mm. Most breaks don’t work, many cafés, hotels and surf camps close, and the parties stop. Come only for a quiet beach and wildlife.

Lahugala · the last hour in

Elephants at Lahugala

The road runs past the tanks of Lahugala–Kitulana National Park, where herds come to the water. Around dawn and dusk you’re very likely to see elephants from the car. Cars can’t go into the park — you watch from the road.

Where to stay

Stay

Stay on the main strip to walk to everything, north towards Whiskey Point for quiet, or south near Elephant Rock for the beginner waves. Many places close from November to March, and June to August books up early.

Luxury

Whisky Point Resort · Jetwing Surf · Arugambay Roccos

Whisky Point Resort’s beachfront bungalows sit right at the break, 20 minutes north of town; Jetwing Surf is the big-name option in the bay; Roccos has an infinity pool and a beach bar. Coming in the off-season? Ask whether they’re open first.

Mid-range

Hideaway · The Spice Trail · Bay Vista

Hideaway is family-run, with 14 rooms in six acres of garden in the middle of the bay and a well-liked restaurant; The Spice Trail is a boutique place with a pool; Bay Vista has an infinity pool and runs surf-and-yoga weeks. Star Rest, between the lagoon and the beach, runs a long-stay surf camp.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

How many nights should I stay?

Two or three for the beach, the lagoon and a lesson or two. If you came to surf, give it a week — the surf camps here sell four-day, week-long and longer packages.

When is the season?

April to September, with June to August the peak — the driest and busiest months. September and October are quieter and cheaper. From November to March it’s the rainy season on this coast: most breaks don’t work and many places close. See the months ↓

Is November to March worth it?

Only if you want an empty beach and some wildlife. The surf is mostly flat, lots of cafés and guesthouses shut, and the parties stop. Check that your guesthouse is open before you book — and for beaches at that time of year, the south coast is the one in season.

Can I visit as a day trip?

Not really. Ella is 3 hours each way and the airport more than 6, and the best of the day here is dawn and sunset. Stay at least two nights.

Is Arugam Bay good for families?

Yes, with care. The waves can be big and the current strong for children, there’s very little shade on the beaches (bring a beach tent), and crocodiles live in the lagoons — keep little ones away from the lagoon edges. The lagoon safari and Kumana are easy days out. Crocodile Rock is a lovely sunset spot, but it drops off quickly: hold hands.

Where do people go after Arugam Bay?

Most go back west to Ella, three hours away, for the hills. If you’re staying on the beach, Trincomalee is the other in-season coast, about 5 h 30 north via Batticaloa. The south coast is in its own season from about December. See the routes ↓

Can a total beginner learn here?

Yes. Baby Point (the inside of Main Point) and Elephant Rock are soft, slow waves where the surf schools teach. A lesson is usually 1.5–2 hours with board and rash vest included, about USD 20–40 at a certified (ISA) school in 2026.

Which surf point is best for me?
  • First time: Baby Point or Elephant Rock, then the inside of Peanut Farm once you’re standing.
  • Linking turns: Whiskey Point — the most consistent wave around.
  • Confident on a reef: Main Point, or Pottuvil Point north of Pottuvil town, a long right over boulders.
  • Want it empty: Okanda at dawn, or Lighthouse near Komari.

Every point works roughly April to September; June to August is the busiest. See the points ↑

How much is board hire?

It varies a lot by shop and board — anything from about Rs 400–800 to USD 5–8 a day. Places like Arne’s Place rent boards to guests and walk-ins.

Is it safe to swim?

Mostly, with care. Watch for rips, especially by the headland at Okanda. Crocodiles live in the lagoon behind Elephant Rock and around Pottuvil Point (more of them from October to March), and are reported at Peanut Farm — swim on the sea side, never in the lagoons. Swim where a surf school or lifeguard says it’s safe.

Do I need a surf guide?

Not for the points around the bay. For Okanda or Lighthouse, a guided surf trip with one of the schools takes care of the transport and the local knowledge.

Is there yoga?

Plenty. Sea-view drop-in classes start at about USD 12.50, and lots of hotels run free classes, some on their rooftops. For a whole week of it, Bay Vista and Paddyway Resort sell surf-and-yoga packages.

Hot and humid all year, and June is the hottest month. There’s very little shade on the sand.

What should I pack?
  • Reef booties
  • A rash vest or shorty
  • High-SPF reef-safe sunscreen
  • A beach shade tent
What should I wear in town?

Swimwear on the sand; a shirt and shorts or a sarong in town. Pottuvil is a conservative town — cover shoulders and knees there and near mosques. Topless sunbathing isn’t allowed anywhere in Sri Lanka.

What about temples?

Cover shoulders and knees, take off shoes and hats, and never pose for a photo with your back to a Buddha statue. Visible Buddha tattoos can get you turned away.

  • Carry cash. There are ATMs in the bay and a Commercial Bank in Pottuvil, but don’t rely on them — the Kumana gate, tuk-tuks and small places want cash.
  • Elephants use the Lahugala road at dusk and after dark. Plan to arrive in daylight — we’ll time your transfer around it — and never ride a scooter on that stretch at night.
  • Renting a scooter needs a Sri Lankan permit: the temporary licence from the airport counter (motorcycles must already be on your home licence; Rs 15,000 for a month), or an international driving permit plus the AA Ceylon recognition permit.
  • Tuk-tuk fares aren’t metered. Agree the price first — about Rs 1,000 return to Peanut Farm.
  • The beach parties run in season only: Thursday at Hideaway, Friday at Sababa on Whiskey Point, Saturday at Mambo’s on the sand at Main Point. They get going around 11 PM.
  • Poya (full-moon) days are dry: bars and shops stop selling alcohol, so a party on a Poya night may not happen at all. Some big hotels may still serve their own guests.
  • Crocodiles live in the lagoons. Watch them from the boat, and keep children back from the water’s edge.
  • From November to March many places close. If you’re coming then, check that your guesthouse, surf school and favourite café are open.
  • June to August is busy. Book your room early.
