Thinking about the train?

Right now the famous Kandy → Ella train doesn’t run. Cyclone Ditwah damaged the line in late 2025; trains from Colombo and Kandy stop short of the hills, and the railway is aiming to reopen the whole line at the end of 2026 or early 2027.

The best bit is running again, though: Nanu Oya → Ella has been back since June 2026, a couple of trains each way a day, about 2 h 30–3 h. Sit on the left. Shorter hops work too — Haputale, Bandarawela or Demodara to Ella. Reserved seats go on sale 30 days ahead, only on seatreservation.railway.gov.lk (you need every passenger’s passport number). Message us and we’ll check seats for your date — and we can drive you to Nanu Oya for it.

Things to see & do

Seven things worth your time

In the order we’d do them. Every one has hours, how far from town, how long to give it, and our honest tip.

1

Nine Arch Bridge

Ella’s landmark and one of the most photographed places in Sri Lanka: a curving viaduct 91 m long and 24 m high, finished in 1921 in stone, brick and cement — the story goes the steel meant for it went to the First World War instead. Set in jungle and tea, it’s best when a blue train rolls across.

From townAbout 20 min on foot from town
OpenAlways open · free by day
Give it1 hour
EffortEasy
From our driversGo before nine — by late morning it’s shoulder to shoulder. Trains are fewer since the line reopened in June, so ask your guesthouse for today’s crossing times. Never stand on the tracks or the bridge for a photo when a train’s due. Too hot to walk? A tuk-tuk goes to the car park above it (≈ Rs 500–800), then it’s ten minutes down.
2

Little Adam’s Peak

The easy one, and one of the gentlest hikes in the country: a path through tea from the Passara road to a 1,141 m summit, then a ridge with views down the Ella Gap and across to Ella Rock. The Flying Ravana zipline runs across the valley below if you want to come down faster.

From townAbout 45 min up from town
OpenFree · go for sunrise
Give it2 hours there and back
EffortEasy
From our driversGo for sunrise, then carry on to Nine Arch Bridge — they’re on the same side of town, so it’s one easy morning walk.
3

Ella Rock

The proper hike: along the railway from Kital Ella station, through eucalyptus and tea, then a steep climb to a cliff edge at around 1,350 m with the whole plain below. Bigger views than Little Adam’s Peak, and a real workout.

From townStart at Kital Ella station, 2 km from town
OpenFree · start by 6 AM
Give it3–4 hours return
EffortChallenging
Good to knowThe paths split a lot. Download the map before you go, or take a local guide (≈ Rs 1,500–3,000) — especially for sunrise, when it’s dark. Friendly people who tell you you’re on the wrong path usually want to be paid to guide you. Proper shoes, water, and leech socks after rain.
4

Ravana Falls

A 25 m waterfall right beside the road down to the plains, tied to the Ramayana: the demon king Ravana is said to have hidden Sita in the caves behind it. Ravana Cave, up a few hundred steps from the temple nearer town, has been lived in for tens of thousands of years.

From town6 km · 15 min by tuk-tuk, on the Wellawaya road
OpenFree · daylight
Give it30–45 min
EffortEasy
Good to knowThe rocks are slippery — admire it from the path. It’s at its best after rain and just a trickle in the driest weeks. Stalls and monkeys at the roadside: keep food in your bag.
5

Diyaluma Falls

Sri Lanka’s second-highest waterfall, 220 m in one long drop. The best part is at the top: a string of natural rock pools you can swim in, with the whole valley below. The best day trip from Ella.

From townAbout 1 h by car, via Wellawaya & Koslanda
OpenFree · daylight
Give itHalf a day
EffortModerate · 30 min down to the upper pools
Good to knowThe trail to the upper pools starts in Poonagala village: loose and uneven going down, harder coming back up. There are no barriers at the drop — stay well back from the edge, and don’t swim when the river is high after heavy rain.

6

Demodara Loop

A clever bit of 1920s railway building: to gain height, the line spirals right round the hill and through a tunnel directly underneath Demodara station — said to be the only loop like it anywhere. Watch a train go under the platform it’s about to stop at.

From town10–15 min by tuk-tuk, or one stop by train
OpenFree
Give it30–45 min
From our driversTake the train one stop from Ella to Demodara: you cross Nine Arch Bridge on the way, and you’re riding the loop instead of looking at it. Tuk-tuk back.
7

A tea factory visit

Ella sits in the middle of the Uva tea country. At Uva Halpewatte, on the hill above town, you watch the leaf withered, rolled, fermented and fired, then taste the result looking over the estate it came from.

From townA short tuk-tuk ride from town
OpenTours 8 AM–4:30 PM
Give it45 min–1 hour
Ticket≈ Rs 3,000 factory tour
From our driversGo in the morning while the machines are running. If you want it smaller and quieter, the Newburgh green-tea factory is a walk from town.
Further afield

Worth a whole day, if you have one

1 h 30each wayLipton’s Seat & Dambatenne

The viewpoint above Haputale where Sir Thomas Lipton surveyed his tea, with five provinces in view on a clear morning. Go early — the cloud comes up by ten. Stop at the Dambatenne tea factory on the way down.

1 heach wayBadulla & Dunhinda Falls

The old provincial town at the end of the railway, with a lively market, the ancient Muthiyangana temple and, just outside, Dunhinda Falls — a wide curtain of spray at the end of a short forest path.

3 heach wayYala, on the way south

Better as a stop than a day trip: head south via Tissamaharama, do an afternoon jeep safari for leopards and elephants, then carry on to the beaches the next day. Tell us your days and we’ll plan the route.

When to come

The weather, month by month

Ella is on the east side of the hills, so its seasons run almost opposite to the south coast’s: dry in the European summer, wettest at the end of the year.

Jan – MarClear & cool

The rains ease through January; February and March have the clearest mornings of the year. Busy and lovely.

Best for views
Apr – MayWarm, some storms

Afternoon thunderstorms between the monsoons. Mid-April is Sinhala & Tamil New Year — buses and trains are packed and some places close.

Jun – SepDry & sunny

The south-west monsoon mostly misses this side of the hills: Ella’s driest months, often windy. The waterfalls are thinner.

Driest
Oct – DecThe rains

The wettest weeks, November most of all. Mornings are still often clear; landslides can close the road down to Wellawaya for a day.

Your questions, answered

The things people ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

How many nights should I stay?

Two or three. That gives you one early morning for each peak, Nine Arch Bridge, a tea factory and Ravana Falls without rushing. Add a night for Diyaluma Falls — it’s the best day out from here.

Can I visit as a day trip?

From Nuwara Eliya it’s possible, but the things worth doing here are best at dawn and spread around the valley. Stay at least two nights.

Which hike is best for me?

If you want easy, choose Little Adam’s Peak: 45 minutes up, paths all the way, fine for children. If you’re fit and want a proper hike, do Ella Rock — 3–4 hours return, steep in places and easy to lose the path. See both ↑

Is Ella good for families?

Yes. Nine Arch Bridge, Ravana Falls, a tea factory and Little Adam’s Peak are all easy at a relaxed pace, and there’s a pizza place children love. Keep hands held near the railway.

Is there any nightlife?

A little. Café One Love stays open latest, with music and a pool table; 360 Ella and Café Chill are good for a drink. Ella is quiet after midnight. Alcohol isn’t sold on full-moon (Poya) days.

Where do people go after Ella?

Most go down to the south coast — Mirissa is three hours — often stopping for a safari at Yala on the way. From May to September, Arugam Bay on the east coast is the one in season. See the routes ↓

Can I take the train from Kandy to Ella?

Not at the moment. The line between Kandy and Nanu Oya was badly damaged in the late-2025 cyclone and is still being rebuilt; the railway is aiming for the end of 2026 or early 2027. We drive you from Kandy in about 3 h 45. See how we get you here ↑

So which trains are running?

Nanu Oya → Ella → Badulla has run again since June 2026, with a couple of trains each way a day on a special timetable. That’s the prettiest part of the line anyway. Times change, so check before you plan around one.

Which side should I sit on?

From Nanu Oya to Ella, sit on the left for the best of the valleys. (When the Kandy section reopens, the right side is better for that stretch.)

Which class should I book?
  • Second class reserved is the sweet spot: windows open, you can stand in the doorway, and your seat is guaranteed.
  • First class air-conditioned is comfier, but the windows don’t open — you’re looking through glass.
  • Observation car, on some trains, faces backwards with big windows. It sells out first.
  • Unreserved is cheapest and bought at the station on the day. Crowded in season, and an experience of its own.
How do I get tickets?

Reserved seats go on sale 30 days ahead on the railway’s own site, seatreservation.railway.gov.lk — the only official one. You need every passenger’s passport number, and seats sell out weeks ahead in season. Don’t buy from resellers at stations or online. Message us on WhatsApp and we’ll help you check what’s left.

What if the train is delayed or cancelled?

It happens, especially in heavy rain. Don’t plan anything tight on the same day. If your plans change, message us and we’ll get you there by road.

What should I pack?
  • Walking shoes with grip
  • A light jumper or jacket
  • A rain jacket
  • Sunscreen & insect repellent
Anything else for the hikes?

Water, a torch if you’re going up for sunrise, and a charged phone with the map downloaded. After rain the trails have leeches — long socks help, and the pharmacies in town sell leech socks.

  • Carry some cash. Tourist cafés take cards, but tuk-tuks, small shops and local places don’t. People’s Bank has an ATM at the station and in town; not every machine takes foreign cards.
  • Tuk-tuk fares aren’t metered. Agree the price first — about Rs 500–800 from town to Nine Arch Bridge. Your guesthouse will tell you what’s fair today.
  • Walking along the tracks is how everyone gets around, and it’s also against the rules. Step well clear when you hear a train, and never stand on Nine Arch Bridge as one comes.
  • Train tickets only from seatreservation.railway.gov.lk or the station. Touts reselling seats at many times the price are a known racket.
  • On Ella Rock, friendly strangers pointing you the ‘right’ way usually want paying. Download the map before you set off.
  • Nine Arch Bridge gets packed on weekends and holidays. Be there before nine.
  • The lanes are steep and dark at night. Take a tuk-tuk back to your guesthouse after dinner.
  • Full-moon (Poya) days: alcohol isn’t sold anywhere and some places close early. Check the date before you plan a night out.
  • Flying a drone needs registering with the Civil Aviation Authority first — police have taken drones near the bridge.
  • Mid-April (Sinhala & Tamil New Year): public transport is packed and many places shut for a few days. Book your car early.
