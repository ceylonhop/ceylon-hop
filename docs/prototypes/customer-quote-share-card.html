The quote link you send the customer

You’re right that the ops link is the wrong thing to share — it’s behind Google sign-in, and its URL is the app, not a document. The customer version is a different link entirely: a public, token-keyed page on ceylonhop.com that expires. Good news — that page and its token are already specified in the deposits design (Slice 1); what’s missing is the unfurl card on top of it.

Don’t make the ops link shareable. Punching a hole in ops.ceylonhop.com auth would be the expensive, risky way to get this. The cheap way is the one already spec’d: when a quote flips to sent, mint a payToken and share ceylonhop.com/quote.html?t=<token>. Ops auth stays untouched.

1 · The card, full size (1200 × 630)

Everything that survives a chat thumbnail: route, dates, who/what/how much, and how long it’s good for. Note what is not here — the day-by-day itinerary. At 1200×630 it turns to mush; the itinerary’s job is to be the payoff when they tap.

Why this beats pasting a PDF

  • The price and validity are visible before they tap — the two things that decide whether they reply.
  • It’s the same CustomerQuoteView the quote email already renders, so the card, the email and the page can never disagree.
  • It’s a live document: if you reprice, the link updates. A PDF forwarded to a partner is stale forever.
  • Forwardable — travelling groups decide in a group chat, and the card carries the pitch into it.
Bags is a new field on the view model. It’s on the quote request (req.bags, and vehicle capacity is maxPax/maxBags) but CustomerQuoteView doesn’t carry it yet — one line to add, and worth it: “4 bags · fits 6” is the reassurance a family actually wants.

3 · The link, and how it expires

Your instinct is right, and the spec already answers it — the expiry isn’t an arbitrary N days, it’s the rate lock. The price is only honest for as long as it’s locked, so the link dies with it. One rule, no second clock to explain.

1
Quote → SentMints a 128-bit random payToken. Never the reference — that’s guessable.
2
Ops copies the linkceylonhop.com/quote.html?t=… from the quote header. Paste into WhatsApp.
3
Lives while lockedValid until rateLockedUntil — 7 days on the current lock rules.
4
Then it softensPast the lock, the page shows “needs re-confirming” with a WhatsApp button. Never 404, never a stale price.
Two other ways a link dies, both status-driven rather than time-driven: the quote is won (→ “you’re booked” state) or lost/expired (→ friendly terminal state). A shared link that’s already paid must never show a pay button again.
The one real trade-off: a token in a URL is a bearer key — anyone the customer forwards it to sees the quote and could pay it. The spec accepts that (WhatsApp transport is encrypted, and the link dies when the quote leaves the payable state). What follows from it: no PII the customer wouldn’t forward themselves — first name and initial, no phone, no email, no internal notes, and never the margin.

4 · The states a shared link has to render

Live quote — the everyday one. Price and “held until” are the two things visible before they tap.

Multi-stop chauffeur trip — endpoints stay big, the chain rides in the teaser. Long routes drop a size automatically.

Last day — saffron, and the phrasing goes plain-language. Still a fixed date underneath, never a countdown.

Expired — the link never 404s. It softens to a re-confirm state so an old forward still lands somewhere warm.

Already paid — a forwarded link must never show a pay button again. Status-gated, not time-gated.

5 · What’s on the card vs. on the page

Card (the thumbnail)Page (the payoff)
RouteEndpoints big; +2 stops chip if multi-stopFull timeline, every stop, nights per stop
ItineraryOne teaser line onlyDay-by-day — reuses the email’s quoteTimeline()
PartyPax · bags · vehicle, one rowSame, plus “fits up to 6 / 6 bags”
PriceOne all-in totalTotal + what’s included. No line-item breakdown — the idle-day naming stays understated, per the existing rule
Validity“Held until Sat 3 Aug” — a fixed dateSame date + what happens after
ActionImplied by the cardPay in full (primary) · WhatsApp us (secondary)
Never put a countdown on the card. WhatsApp fetches a preview once and caches it against the URL for days — “expires in 3 days” would freeze and lie. The Ride Board card already learned this; same rule, fixed dates only.
