The quote page

The design for quote.ceylonhop.com/q?t=…, per docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-05-customer-quote-link-design.md. Follows on from the 28 July share-card prototype, which designed the unfurl card that sits on top of this page. Every class and value in the mockups below is lifted verbatim from site.css and ticket.css — .pp-hello in brand teal, pill buttons, the .hops rail, .t-stat pills, the .st-wrap keepsake — so this is the page, not an impression of it.

One deliberate divergence from the pay page (spec D13). No perforation, no stub, no tear line. Those are a boarding pass — they say "you have bought this". A quote has not been bought, and dressing a proposal as a ticket quietly implies otherwise. Same design language, plain .ticket cards; tearing off a stub stays the visual reward for paying. The column also widens 470 → 660px — the pay page caps narrow because it has one job, and this page's job needs room for a map.
No pay button anywhere on this page (spec D6). Every option's action is WhatsApp. That is what makes a forwarded link harmless, and it is why the page can afford to show a price at all.

Stacked on a phone. Both cards are complete: price, inclusions and cancellation terms each stand alone, because a customer will screenshot one and forward it.

Why the second card isn't a footnote

Today the chauffeur price reaches the customer as a sentence appended to a WhatsApp message by appendChauffeurUpsell() — after the real quote, in the same grey as everything else. It reads as an afterthought, so it gets treated as one.

Given equal visual weight, the more expensive option stops being an upsell and becomes a choice. The lead card keeps a quiet ring rather than a "RECOMMENDED" badge: you priced it, so it leads — but the page shouldn't pressure a decision you were happy to leave open. Its button is the house .btn-wa pill; the second card's is .btn-ghost, the same pair every primary/secondary moment on the site already uses.

Cancellation terms sit on each card, not in a footer — pay.html's dt-pol idiom: headline rule always visible, full ladder one tap away. The two ladders genuinely differ — 24 hours versus 10 days, 100% versus 80% — and a customer comparing $840 with $1,180 is comparing two different commitments.
Degrades cleanly to one card. On a both quote where the trip is single-day or has an undated leg, serviceChooserData can't price chauffeur. The page then renders exactly panel 1 — one option, full width, no hole where the second card was.

What lapsing does and doesn't do

Lapsed
The linkStill works. Liveness is the quote's status, not the clock (D8).
The itineraryUnchanged and still shown in full — it's the part that took the work.
The priceShown, dated, visibly past. Never silently re-priced under the customer.
The actionBecomes "get an up-to-date price" — one WhatsApp tap.
OpsCan still mint a pay link and honour the old price. Lapsing gates this page's display, never your judgement (§7).
Re-approving resets it. Seven more days from the new approval, so the fix for a lapsed quote is the thing ops was going to do anyway.

The fifth state needs no new art

unavailable — a bad token, a soft-deleted quote, a status that isn't payable — renders the existing "This quote has sailed off somewhere sunny" screen from pay.html, illustration and all. It's already written, already tested, and already the right tone.

Deliberately soft. Like /quote-pay/view, an unverifiable token returns 200 with {state:'unavailable'} — no distinction between "wrong token" and "quote withdrawn", so the endpoint can't be used to probe which quotes exist.

5 · What this page does not have

Not hereWhy
A pay buttonSpec D6. The money path is unreachable from a quote link, which is what makes forwarding it harmless.
The perforated ticketA boarding pass says "bought". This is a proposal (D13).
A line-item breakdownOne total per option. Idle-day pricing stays deliberately understated — a stay_day is named, never priced.
A countdownFixed dates only. WhatsApp caches the unfurl per URL for days; a countdown would freeze and lie.
Any editable fieldSpec D5 — the customer reads, ops writes.
Per-day proseNo such field exists in the data (§5.1). Every day row above is generated from category, date, from, to, distanceKm and stops[].
The one thing worth arguing about. "Colombo Airport → Sigiriya · 168 km · about 4 h" is accurate and slightly mechanical. One ops-authored line per day — "straight up to the cultural triangle; you'll want the afternoon free for the rock" — would change what this page is. That's a new field plus ops UI, and it's the single highest-leverage addition to this design. Currently out of scope.
