The places

Where the story is remembered

In the order we’d visit them, with timings, steps and our honest tip. These are places of tradition and belief, not proven history, and in Sri Lanka Ravana is also respected as a great king, so we tell both sides of the story.

Illustrative photo 1

Seetha Amman Kovil · Ashok Vatika

Tradition holds that this is Ashok Vatika, the garden where Ravana kept Sita and where Hanuman found her and gave her Rama’s ring. Today it is a living Hindu temple beside a stream at Seetha Eliya, on the road from Nuwara Eliya to Hakgala. The temple was re-consecrated in May 2024 with water brought from the Sarayu at Ayodhya.

WhereSeetha Eliya · 5–8 km from Nuwara Eliya
Open8 am–1 pm · 2–6:30 pm
EntryFree · puja about LKR 100–200
StepsShort, flat walk
Our tipCome for the morning puja, before the coaches. The floor is cold at 1,800 m, so bring thick socks for after you take your shoes off. Skip the boulders by the stream with older parents.
Illustrative photo 2

Shri Bhakta Hanuman Temple, Ramboda

Tradition holds that Hanuman passed through these hills searching for Sita. The Chinmaya Mission temple (2002) has an 18-foot Hanuman looking out over the tea, and a vegetarian restaurant.

WhereOn the A5, Kandy → Nuwara Eliya
Open7 am–1 pm · 3:30–6:30 pm
StepsAbout 60, with seats
PhotosNot of the idol
Our tipArrive before 1 pm and have lunch here. The temple restaurant is a good vegetarian lunch stop.
Illustrative photo 3

Munneswaram & Manavari, Chilaw

Tradition holds that Rama installed a lingam at Manavari and worshipped Shiva at Munneswaram after the war, to atone for killing Ravana. Both are active temples. Munneswaram’s month-long festival in August–September draws Hindu, Buddhist, Catholic and Muslim pilgrims.

WhereChilaw · about 1–1½ h from the airport
EntryFree
StepsRoadside, flat
Good forDay 1, straight off the flight
Illustrative photo 4

Divurumpola, near Welimada

Tradition holds that Sita took her agni pariksha, the trial by fire, here. Today it is a Buddhist temple, with a Bo tree, stupas and statues of Rama, Sita and Hanuman. Offerings follow Buddhist custom.

WhereAbout 4 km off the Nuwara Eliya–Welimada road
StepsFlat, with benches
5

Ravana Falls, Ella

Tradition holds that Sita was hidden in caves behind the falls. You see it from the roadside on the Ella–Wellawaya road, so it’s an easy stop for everyone.

Where6 km below Ella
EntryFree

More places on the trail

6Nuwara EliyaHakgala Botanical Garden

The other place claimed as Ashok Vatika, on the same road as Seetha Amman, so visiting both settles the question. Shady, easy paths; foreign adult entry LKR 3,540.

7Nuwara EliyaGayathri Peedam

Where tradition places Indrajit’s ritual before battle. Active shrine 3 km from town, about 80 steps.

8ColomboKelaniya Temple

Where Vibhishana is said to have been crowned. A Buddhist temple with coronation murals, 10 km from the city. About 50 steps.

9ColomboPanchamuga Anjaneyar

Five-faced Hanuman at Kalubowila. A devotional stop, not a legend site. Good for your last morning.

10Deep southKataragama

Where Skanda is said to have helped Rama. Hindu, Buddhist and Muslim pilgrims share the evening puja.

11AmbalantotaUssangoda

Red earth said to be scorched by Hanuman’s burning tail. A national park on an open plateau by the sea.

12UnawatunaRumassala

A fragment of the Sanjeevani mountain Hanuman carried. A coastal hill 20 min from Galle Fort.

Ravana Cave, Ella: fit travellers onlyThe cave tradition says Sita was hidden in is up 500–800 steep, often slippery steps, and it’s dark with bats inside. Your driver will wait while the younger ones climb; we don’t put it in the plan for elders.

At the temples · on the road

Temples, food and comfort

  • Dress: shoulders and knees covered, shoes off at every temple. Some shrines have their own customs, so follow what others do or ask.
  • Food: tell us vegetarian, Jain or no onion-garlic and we’ll suggest lunch stops that fit.
  • Cold floors: Nuwara Eliya is at 1,868 m. Pack shawls and thick socks.
  • Hairpins: the Kandy–Nuwara Eliya road is winding. Carsick-prone? Your driver will stop whenever you need.
  • Buddhist sites: Divurumpola and Kelaniya are Buddhist temples. Dress modestly, take off shoes and hats, and ask at the temple how offerings are made.
Your questions, answered

The things families ask us

Pick a topic, tap a question.

Is Ashok Vatika real?

Tradition places it at Seetha Eliya, where the Seetha Amman temple stands today; some say Hakgala gardens next door. They’re on the same road, so we take you to both.

How far is Ashok Vatika from Colombo and Kandy?

About 2 h 45 from Kandy by road, and about 5 h from Colombo or the airport. That’s why we break the journey in Kandy.

Can we travel from India by Ram Setu?

No. There’s no road or ferry across it. Fly to Colombo, or take the Nagapattinam–Kankesanthurai ferry to Jaffna. You can see where Ram Setu begins from Talaimannar, in Mannar.

Is it suitable for elderly parents?

Yes, that’s who the 6-day trip is built for. Most sites are flat or under 80 steps. We leave Ravana Cave and Sigiriya out for elders and keep every day short.

What are Seetha Amman temple timings?

8 am–1 pm and 2–6:30 pm. Entry is free; a puja is about LKR 100–200.

Can you arrange a puja?

Tell us when you ask for a quote and we’ll check what each temple offers for your dates.

Can we take photos?

Outside, yes. Not of the Hanuman idol at Ramboda, and ask before photographing inside any shrine.

What does the price include?

Your own air-conditioned car or van and a driver for every day of the trip, with fuel, tolls and parking. Hotels, entry fees, meals and offerings are paid separately, so you only pay for what you choose.

Can we change the plan?

Yes. Add a day or drop a stop and we’ll requote.

How do we pay?

A secure card link in USD. A small deposit holds your dates and the rest is due before you travel.
